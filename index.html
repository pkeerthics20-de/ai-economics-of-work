AI Won't Just Change Jobs.
It Will Change the Economics of Work.

The interesting question isn't simply whether AI can do our work. It's how AI changes the amount of human effort required to deliver the same outcome.

By Keerthi • Data Engineer • Technology Enthusiast

🤔 The Big Question

There is a lot of discussion about AI replacing humans. But I think there is a more practical way to look at it.

"If AI can do a significant portion of the work, how will the economics of a team change?"

Let's take a simple example from a typical enterprise Data & Analytics project.

🏢 One Project. Three Possible Models.

Imagine a company needs an end-to-end data and analytics solution.

🔵 Traditional Team

5 people

  • 1 SME
  • 2 Data Engineers
  • 2 Power BI Developers

High human effort with limited automation.

🟣 AI-Heavy

AI Agents

  • Code generation
  • Testing
  • Documentation
  • Automation

Very high automation, but still requires human oversight.

🟢 Hybrid

3 people + AI

  • 1 SME
  • 1 Data Engineer
  • 1 BI Developer
  • AI Agents

Human expertise combined with AI execution.

💰 The Economics

Suppose the traditional team represents approximately ₹50–80 lakh in annual employee CTC.

Illustrative traditional team cost
₹50–80 Lakhs

Employee CTC is not the same as client billing. A services company also has infrastructure, management, administration, benefits, sales and margins.

Now imagine AI allows the company to deliver the same solution with three people instead of five.

The company may not think: "AI is replacing humans."

It may simply think: "Why should I pay for five people when three people with AI can deliver the same outcome?"

⚡ What AI Really Changes

Traditional AI + Humans
Team Size Higher Potentially Smaller
Repetitive Work High Much Lower

Execution Speed Normal Faster
Human Focus Execution Decision + Expertise

🧠 Why Domain Expertise Still Matters

A client doesn't approach a technology company simply because they cannot write SQL.

They come because they have a business problem and need someone to translate that problem into a technology solution.

AI can write the SQL. AI can generate the pipeline. AI can create the documentation. AI can assist with testing.

But someone still needs to understand:

  • What the business actually needs
  • What the data actually means
  • Which business rules should apply
  • Whether the output is correct
  • What should happen when something fails
  • Who owns the final decision

🚀 The Takeaway

I'm not trying to predict whether AI will replace humans.

I'm looking at what AI means from a business and cost perspective.

If AI can reduce the effort required to deliver a solution, companies will naturally look for ways to use it.

The future may not be
Humans vs AI.

It may be:
Humans + AI
vs
Humans without AI.

The people who adapt, integrate AI into their work and deepen their expertise in their domain may be able to use AI as a productivity multiplier.

So don't just learn AI.
Deepen your domain.
Understand your business.
Learn how AI fits into your work.

Domain Expertise + AI = Leverage

Note: The cost figures are illustrative examples intended to explain the economic concept. Actual employee costs, AI infrastructure costs and client billing vary significantly by organisation, geography, contract and workload.
